# Sustainability and AI energy: what HPE says in its FY2025 10-K

## Living Progress strategy

Item 1 of HPE's FY2025 10-K has an "Environmental Sustainability" section:

> "Living Progress is our business strategy for creating more sustainable and responsible IT solutions that meet the technology demands of the future, while advancing the way people live and work. Anchored to our edge-to-cloud offerings and bolstered by our sustainability credentials, our Living Progress strategy provides us with a competitive advantage in the market as we help our customers pursue their business objectives through more efficient and sustainable practices."

It adds that "The HPE Board of Directors, including through its committees, provides oversight of our Living Progress strategy, risks, practices, policies, and disclosures."

## AI and energy consumption

HPE links AI directly to rising energy demand:

> "In 2025, the majority of our greenhouse gas emissions resulted from our customers' use of our products and solutions. The rising demand for compute-intensive technologies—particularly those that support artificial intelligence—is contributing to increased energy consumption across the technology sector, presenting decarbonization challenges for leading solutions providers such as HPE."

> "Accordingly, we are committed to delivering products and solutions that may help our customers minimize the electricity consumption and carbon footprints of their IT estates while also enhancing productivity, and potentially reducing costs. For instance, the latest generation of HPE ProLiant Gen 12 Servers enables customers to consolidate and grow compute capabilities, while potentially lowering the use and cost of power and cooling."

HPE notes that "many of our products are certified by eco-labels, such as the Electronic Product Environment Assessment Tool, Energy STAR, China SEPA, and the China Energy Conservation Program."

## Liquid cooling and efficient AI systems

The 10-K strategy section cites "technologies like fanless direct liquid cooling that power our largest AI systems." It describes HPE ProLiant servers and rack-scale solutions as delivering "sustainable accelerated computing for training and inferencing." Server R&D includes "innovative direct liquid cooling." Hewlett Packard Labs' research agenda includes "sustainability" alongside AI, networking, novel accelerators and quantum computing.

The June 2024 HPE/NVIDIA release described Private Cloud AI as offering "an energy-efficient, fast and flexible path for sustainably developing and deploying generative AI applications." It said HPE GreenLake cloud management "includes sustainability metrics for workloads and endpoints." In networking, the Q3 FY2026 call cited a "direct liquid cooled Tomahawk 6 base switch."

## Circular economy through Financial Services

"Through our Technology Renewal Centers, we are helping customers achieve their own sustainability goals by refurbishing technology assets for reuse." Financial Services helps customers "achieve sustainability goals."

## Climate-related risk factors

- "Risks arising from climate change and the transition to a lower-carbon economy may impact our business." Physical risk: HPE's headquarters in Spring, Texas "suffers from floods, hurricanes" and other events.
- Transition risk: "We continue to observe increased customer demand for more sustainable and energy-efficient solutions, products, and services ... We may also confront higher electricity prices, and higher costs for supplies or components that comply with certain environmental regulatory thresholds, potentially impacting our margins or the pricing of our offerings."
- Goal risk: the Living Progress strategy "is based on available data and estimates, and is not a guarantee that we will be able to achieve such plans and aspirations." Achieving it depends partly on "the ability and willingness of our suppliers to take action and the advancement of new technologies."

## What this source does not contain

The 10-K does not state specific emissions figures, net-zero target years, or PUE/energy-efficiency numbers for HPE AI systems. Those would be in HPE's standalone Living Progress report, which was not retrieved, so none are included here.
